This year, I chose to move closer.
Not only to the musicians, but also to the audience—to the sweat, the pushing shoulders, and the noise that makes the room feel even smaller. I focused on small gigs, intimate venues where distance barely exists and everything feels more honest.

With flash and a wide lens, I photograph from very close range—sometimes too close. Tight crowds and constant movement force me to slow down, to read the flow of bodies around me, and to find space without interrupting how people experience the music.

I slowly learned that the key to photographing small gigs is becoming part of the crowd itself. I push myself to stand very close to the musicians, and to step into the middle of the audience while they’re moshing. Getting bumped, pulled, losing balance—these risks become part of the process of seeing and capturing the moment.

I’ve always been drawn to concert atmosphere. A wide lens helps me frame narrow spaces, exploding energy, and the almost non-existent distance between the stage and the crowd. In small gigs, all of that feels complete.

Maybe this year is no longer just about metal. I’ve started photographing pop, hardcore, screamo, punk, and punk rock—genres that often feel most alive in small, intimate spaces. Spaces where music doesn’t just sound loud, but collides with bodies.

In the middle of it all, I’m not just documenting.
I’m inside it.
